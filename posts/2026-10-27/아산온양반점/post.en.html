Buckwheat Noodles and Crispy Tangsuyuk at Onyang Banjeom in Asan, Korea

There's a special kind of joy in finding a neighborhood Chinese-Korean restaurant that does something a little different. Last weekend in Asan, a quiet city in South Chungcheong Province, I stopped by Onyang Banjeom (온양반점) for lunch, mostly because I'd heard one thing about it: the noodles here are made with buckwheat. Spoiler: the noodles were interesting, but the star of the table turned out to be something else entirely. 🥢

Tangsuyuk: fried to order, and it shows

Tangsuyuk (탕수육) is Korea's take on sweet-and-sour pork: strips of pork in a light batter, fried until crisp and paired with a glossy sauce of vegetables and fruit. Ours arrived clearly fresh from the fryer. The coating was thin and shatteringly crisp, and the pork inside stayed juicy.

The sauce came in a separate bowl, which matters more than you'd think. Koreans love to debate bu-meok (pour the sauce over) versus jjik-meok (dip each piece). I ate the first few plain to enjoy the crunch, then switched to dipping. Honestly, this plate alone would bring me back.

Jjajangmyeon with a buckwheat twist

Jjajangmyeon (짜장면) is probably the most beloved comfort food in Korea: noodles topped with a thick, savory-sweet black bean sauce full of diced onion, potato and pork. Here the sauce was poured on generously, with chunky vegetables you could eat by the spoonful.

Mix it up and you'll notice the difference right away: the noodles are a grey-brown color because they're made with buckwheat. They're a bit less chewy and more rustic than the usual wheat noodles, with a nutty, earthy flavor that pairs surprisingly well with the rich sauce. If you're used to springy noodles it may feel unusual at first, but I found it a nice change.

Jjamppong: clams and comfort

Jjamppong (짬뽕) is the spicy sibling of jjajangmyeon: a red seafood noodle soup. This bowl came piled with clams, cabbage and onion, and it looked promisingly fiery.

The noodles are buckwheat here as well. To be honest, though, the broth tasted less like a deep, smoky seafood jjamppong and more like a familiar bowl of Korean instant ramen. That isn't necessarily a bad thing; it's easy and comforting to slurp. But if you're chasing a bold, smoky jjamppong, I'd point you toward the jjajangmyeon and tangsuyuk instead.

Good to know

💡 Order the tangsuyuk and ask for the sauce on the side so you can enjoy the crunch first.
💡 Every noodle dish here uses buckwheat noodles, so expect a nuttier, less chewy texture.
💡 If you're sharing, jjajangmyeon plus tangsuyuk is the winning combination.

The verdict

Onyang Banjeom is a neighborhood Chinese-Korean spot with a real personality, thanks to its buckwheat noodles. The freshly fried tangsuyuk is the must-order, the jjajangmyeon is a satisfying, earthy take on a classic, and the jjamppong is easygoing rather than intense. If you find yourself in Asan around lunchtime, it's well worth a stop. Happy eating! 🧡
